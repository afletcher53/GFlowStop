# Claims and approach

## Status of this document

This note describes the synthetic benchmark generated by
`python -m gflowstop.toy_experiment`. The reviewed result bundle records
source revision `6a1935625461169103545177ba7d490ce314f701`, a clean source
tree, hashes of both benchmark source files, and a hash of the fixed-data
manifest in `results/toys/metadata.json`.

The project name is historical. The current benchmark is **not a GFlowNet
stopping result**. It is a cost-sensitive Bayesian stopping benchmark with
exact small-horizon planning and policy distillation.

## What was attempted under the GFlowNet name

The original fixed-chain proposal assigned terminal targets using the realised
topic world and tried to learn stop/continue behaviour from flow-like targets.
That is not valid value-of-information training: the current decision does not
know the realised world. It also produces a structural horizon bias because a
fixed chain exposes more terminal stopping depths after continuation.

The perfect-information regression makes the distinction executable. With a
single label that reveals the world, the Bayes-optimal policy continues, while
the hindsight target stops immediately with the answer it was not entitled to
observe. See `test_hindsight_flow_target_is_not_bayes_optimal`.

Consequently, the repository must not claim that a GFlowNet has solved this
stopping problem. A genuine future GFlowNet line would need either posterior
sampling over latent relevance configurations or an evidence-acquisition DAG
in which different document-selection orders merge into the same evidence
state.

## Problems actually evaluated

### Track U: threshold-decision utility

For a fixed ranked pool with binary labels `y_1, ..., y_D`, the latent world is

```
W = 1[sum(y_k) >= K].
```

At each reviewed depth the policy chooses `continue`, `accept`, or `reject`.
Terminal utility depends on the decision threshold `q*`; each reviewed document
costs `c`, reported through the horizon-normalised quantity `kappa = cD`.
The primary Track-U result is realised net utility, not recall.

### Track R: target-recall stopping

Track R asks whether a target recall has been reached while minimising unique
documents reviewed. Its primary outputs are premature-stop failure rate,
one-sided upper confidence bound on that rate, review fraction, overshoot, and
effort above the hindsight recall-depth lower bound. Track-U utility policies
do not receive recall-certification claims, and Track-R methods are not judged
by Track-U utility alone.

## Implementation

`src/gflowstop/toys.py` provides:

- fixed episodes that separate labels/scores from evaluation-only latent data;
- perfect-information, beta-binomial, rank-decay, delayed-information,
  clustered, mixture-shift, and sparse-recall generators;
- exact enumeration for short horizons;
- a rank-binned latent-intensity belief model with optional topic-score-mean
  and local score components;
- full-horizon and truncated belief planning;
- hard logical certificates for already-attained and unattainable thresholds.

`src/gflowstop/toy_experiment.py` writes the episodes, raw per-topic outcomes,
condition summaries, calibration summaries, planner diagnostics, recall
diagnostics, gates, and source/data provenance. The 10,000-topic recall cells
are intentionally retained for the failure-rate precision they provide.

## Validated synthetic findings

### Exactness and matched specification

The beta-binomial sequence enumeration equals the Count-Bellman recursion to
numerical precision. On beta-binomial data, Count-Bellman is competitive with
the true-parameter teacher and the amortised count student remains slightly
below the teacher. For example, at 500 training topics the fitted teacher has
mean utility `0.11314`, the true-parameter teacher `0.11172`, and the student
`0.11144` on this finite held-out sample.

This supports the narrow claim that the count-state solver is coherent when
the exchangeable model is correct. It does not establish strictly monotone
learning curves or general superiority: finite-sample fitted values can be
above or below the true-model value on a realised test set.

### Rank misspecification and the rank-aware repair

The informative rank-decay condition is `gamma=1.5`, where the true oracle
continues at the root for 58.9% of topics and its mean root information value
is `0.03203`. Mean utilities are:

| Method | Mean utility |
| --- | ---: |
| True rank oracle | 0.17586 |
| Rank + score mean | 0.15828 |
| Rank + real scores | 0.15776 |
| Rank only | 0.14856 |
| Count-Bellman | 0.10948 |

Relative to the true rank oracle, real-score rank planning removes 72.7% of
the Count-Bellman utility regret in this cell. This supports a synthetic,
model-specific claim: exchangeability is materially harmful under rank-decay,
and a rank-aware observation model repairs much of the resulting decision
loss.

The score ablation narrows that interpretation. Within-topic score shuffling
has utility `0.15824`, nearly identical to real scores, while local-score-only
planning has `0.14766`. Topic-score-mean-only planning has `0.15828`.
Therefore the observed gain is chiefly topic-level yield information, not
evidence that local rank-by-rank score alignment improves stopping.

### Non-myopia

In the delayed-information toy, one- and two-step lookahead stop immediately
with mean utility `0.00100`. Five-step and full ten-step lookahead both reach
`0.17965`. This supports the claim that some stopping environments have
jointly valuable blocks of observations that one-step EVSI misses.

The rank-aware full-horizon planner has an additional independent exactness
test: on a known three-intensity, eight-rank model it matches the enumerated
POMDP Q-values at every reachable history to `1e-10`.

### Recall safety and effort

Claimed-safe methods are evaluated with a one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper
bound separately for every condition; the gate uses the worst cell rather than
a pooled mean. On the ideal random-order toy, QBCB has observed failure rates
from 0.23% to 1.55% across the evaluated targets and confidence levels, while
reviewing 93.2% to 99.3% of the pool. The conservative sequential
hypergeometric comparator has zero observed failures in 10,000 reviews, whose
upper bound is `0.00030`, but reviews at least 96.5% of the pool.

On the sparse five-positive, 500-document toy, the safe comparators fall back
to full screening. Consecutive-miss-20 reviews about 5.0% of the pool and
fails on every episode. This is evidence for the expected safety-effort
trade-off, not a broad recall-certification claim.

## Claims that are not supported

- No claim that a GFlowNet solves stopping, or that the present policy is a
  GFlowNet.
- No claim of real-collection generalisation, universal method superiority, or
  deployable recall certification from the utility policy.
- No claim that local retrieval scores drive the current rank-aware gain.
- No claim that the simple monotone residual estimator is published QuantCI;
  it is labelled `Monotone-rank-plug-in-UCB`.
- No claim that the simplified exponential point-process comparator reproduces
  a published point-process procedure.
- No scalability claim for exact rank-aware full-horizon planning beyond the
  small horizons tested here.

## Next evidence required before real-data promotion

1. Expand only decision-live cells. The new root diagnostics identify
   `gamma=1.5` and `gamma=3.0` as useful starting regimes; `gamma=0` and
   `gamma=0.5` are primarily prior decisions.
2. Add multiple thresholds, utility thresholds, and costs while retaining
   paired true-oracle regret for small horizons.
3. Add clustered variants with informative, weak, and uninformative scores to
   isolate label dependence from score leakage.
4. Implement published recall procedures faithfully before interpreting them
   as literature baselines, then widen prevalence, ranking quality, target,
   and calibration-shift conditions.
5. Only after planner and belief-model gates hold, compare policy
   approximation and training objectives. Real collections come after those
   synthetic gates, not before.

The Repke external target-recall adapter has now completed a matched screen on
the released 81-dataset-ID subset against the framework's direct CMH baseline.
At 80% recall, rank-only GFlowStop-R fails the safety gate (19.82% failures;
24.80% one-sided 95% upper bound), whereas CMH has 0.46% failures and a 2.17%
upper bound. At 90% and 95%, CMH also uses less review effort at the same
aggregate confidence level. The optional local-score ablation degrades safety.
This freezes the current recall-policy line as a negative result; no real-data
superiority or recall-certification claim follows. Its approximation,
provenance, and remaining Track-U gate are in `EXTERNAL_BENCHMARK.md`.

The first Track-U external diagnostic is a constructed CLEF-IP K=1 mixed
split: each partition combines 500 positive CLEF-IP topics with 500
corpus-sampled structural-zero controls. The exact zero-inflated rank planner
has mean utility `0.13581`, below the directly called external Greedy policy's
`0.17137` (paired difference `-0.03556`, SE `0.00616`). A score-mean prior
reaches `0.24183`, but its root-world AUC is `0.99907` and it continues on
only 4% of topics. That gain is a construction-signature diagnostic: raw
mean BM25 scores identify the generated controls. It is not evidence of
planning, natural negative-world generalisation, or superiority over the
published decision-theoretic work. The exact K=1 solver is validated; the
external claim remains unpromoted.
